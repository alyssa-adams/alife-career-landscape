4The question this whole exercise exists for

An honest caveat. You just did this retrospectively, and retrospective recall of how things felt is biased — peak-end effects, mood-congruent recall, duration neglect. That is an argument for running the real version prospectively for two or three weeks. Not against the exercise.

6Three futures — an abbreviated Odyssey Plan

Hold all three in parallel. Do not rank them. In quality-diversity terms this is an archive of elites, and keeping the archive diverse is what makes the search find the good solution.
A · What you're already doing
B · If that vanished tomorrow
Your subfield gets solved, defunded, or automated.
C · If money and other people's opinion were no object

If you only say one of these out loud, say C. It's the one nobody says.

7Your compass — take an hour on these, separately

Workview (~250 words): what is work for? Not what job you want. What defines worthwhile work? What does money have to do with it?
Lifeview (~250 words): what is a life for? What is the relationship between you and others? What is the role of joy, sorrow, love, strife?
Then read them side by side. Where do they clash? The compass is the coherence, not either document alone.

8When to pivot

Not when you're unhappy. Happiness is a noisy, slow, confounded signal.

Leave when your rate of improvement falls to the average rate available to you elsewhere. That's Charnov's marginal value theorem, 1976 — and note it's the derivative, not the level. Counterintuitively: the richer the environment, the sooner you should leave.

And when you go, take your parts with you. Across 58,000 startups, what separated eventual success from stagnation was reuse — the people who made it discarded less between attempts. Thrashing looks exactly like pivoting decisively.
